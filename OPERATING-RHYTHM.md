# Operating Rhythm

The registers are most useful when they support ordinary management rather than create a separate governance ceremony.

## Weekly or fortnightly: active work

Review the project register from top to bottom.

For each active row ask only:

1. Is the status still accurate?
2. What is the next milestone and date?
3. Is there an action, dependency or risk that needs intervention?

Update the row during the meeting where possible.

Do not convert routine task lists into projects. A project/workstream should be material enough that the organisation benefits from seeing it in the shared view.

## Monthly: risks and policies

### Risks

Review material risks, especially:

- new risks;
- high residual exposure;
- actions due before the next review;
- controls that no longer work as expected;
- risks whose cause or operating context has changed.

Closed risks should leave the live register rather than accumulate indefinitely.

### Policies

Scan the policy register for:

- review dates approaching;
- missing evidence/controls;
- owner changes;
- obligations that have materially changed.

Do not reread every policy every month. The register exists to make exceptions visible.

## At the point of decision

Record a material decision when it is made.

A decision is usually worth recording when one or more of these applies:

- meaningful money or commitment is involved;
- a material risk is accepted or reduced;
- an option is deliberately rejected;
- the decision will matter after the people in the room forget the discussion;
- somebody may reasonably ask later, “Why did we do this?”

The decision register is not meeting minutes. Routine operational choices do not belong in it.

## Quarterly: archive and simplify

Review the live registers and remove noise.

Archive:

- completed/cancelled projects;
- risks that no longer exist;
- superseded policies;
- decisions that are no longer operationally useful in the live view.

Then ask whether any field is routinely empty or any additional field is being maintained without changing a decision. If so, simplify.

## Escalation test

A spreadsheet or Markdown register stops being enough when the organisation genuinely needs capabilities such as:

- many concurrent editors and complex permissions;
- automated workflow/approvals;
- regulated immutable records;
- high-volume dependencies;
- integration with operational systems;
- automated notifications and evidence capture;
- reporting that cannot be produced reliably from the simple register.

That is the point to consider a platform. The trigger is operational need, not the existence of software that can do more.
